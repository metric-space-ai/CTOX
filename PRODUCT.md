# Product

## Product Register

- **Workjet** is the user-facing desktop, mobile and web application.
- **CTOX** is the self-hosted runtime, backend, sync engine and open-source app
  platform used by Workjet.
- **Business OS** is a Workjet workspace powered by CTOX, not a separate
  competing product name.

Technical protocol, database, crate and API identifiers keep their existing
CTOX names.

## Users

Workjet's Business OS workspace is used by founders, administrators,
operators, specialists, and business teams who need to work directly with
records, queues, documents,
approvals, and automations. It is also used by coding agents that create or
modify Business OS apps through the same governed platform contracts.

Users work in dense operational contexts. They need to understand current
scope, permissions, sync state, selected records, pending work, and the next
available action without navigating through decorative dashboards or separate
configuration sites.

## Product Purpose

Workjet makes typical business and productivity apps quick to create, safe to
distribute, and predictable to operate. Apps remain flexible HTML, CSS, and
JavaScript surfaces while the CTOX platform supplies local-first
persistence, synchronization, authorization, commands, delegation, release
governance, window management, and reusable interaction patterns.

Success means a person or coding agent can start from a small number of proven
app archetypes, implement the domain-specific workflow, and deliver an app that
already behaves consistently in windowed and maximized modes, light and dark
themes, permission-limited states, mobile-sheet presentation, and real browser
workflows.

## Brand Personality

Precise, compact, capable.

The interface should feel like a dependable professional instrument. It is
quiet during routine work, immediate under repeated use, and confidently
visible when an app exposes its unique automation or decision point.

## Anti-references

- Glossy dark SaaS dashboards with radial gradients, ambient glow, glass cards,
  oversized radii, and heavy shadows.
- Landing-page composition inside authenticated apps: heroes, marketing copy,
  large metric mosaics, or decorative card grids.
- Generic AI interfaces that foreground chat while hiding records, scope,
  permissions, commands, or results.
- App-specific reinventions of import, search, filter, sort, edit, save,
  context menus, dialogs, tables, or run status.
- Dense interfaces that achieve compactness by sacrificing focus visibility,
  contrast, keyboard access, label clarity, or readable state.

## Design Principles

1. **The tool disappears into the work.** Routine controls remain familiar,
   compact, and spatially stable across apps.
2. **One app, one operating surface.** Apps expose real records, queues,
   editors, timelines, or automations immediately and remain usable in a
   responsive window.
3. **Color is operational.** Color communicates selection, status, permission,
   risk, or the app's single signature action. It is not ambient decoration.
4. **Context survives every action.** Selection, record identity, permissions,
   window state, delegation, and run evidence remain visible and durable.
5. **Platform behavior is shared behavior.** Common UI, authorization,
   persistence, commands, loading, and release rules belong to the platform,
   not to individual apps.
6. **Density creates responsive capacity.** Routine UI consumes only the space
   needed for repeated work. Touch enlarges hit areas without turning every
   control into a visual hero; only a real app-specific AI automation may be
   the single signature action of its visible work surface.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The target is WCAG 2.2 AA for supported Business OS workflows. Every essential
action must be keyboard accessible with visible focus. Meaning must not depend
on color alone. Reduced-motion preferences are respected, light and dark themes
must both meet the contrast target, and controls must tolerate German and
English label expansion at every supported window width.
